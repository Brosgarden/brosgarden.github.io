A Chinese-inspired roast pork dinner built around warm five-spice aromatics, a tangy Zhenjiang black vinegar glaze, blistered green beans with chili crisp, and fragrant jasmine rice. A savory soy pan sauce brings everything together without making the meal overly sweet.

Active prep is about 20 minutes, with an optional 4–12-hour dry brine. Cook time in the header (~90 min) is approximate elapsed oven and stovetop time; roast shape, oven preheat, and when you start the sides will shift the total. Expect about 1 hour 45 minutes–2 hours 15 minutes start to finish, not counting the dry brine.

## Ingredients

### Five-Spice Pork Loin

- 785 g (1.73 lb) boneless pork loin roast
- 5 g kosher salt
- 2 g Chinese five-spice powder
- 5 g neutral oil
- 8 g fresh ginger, finely grated
- 5 g garlic, finely grated

#### Black Vinegar Glaze

- 12 g light soy sauce
- 15 g Shaoxing wine or sake
- 8 g Zhenjiang (Chinkiang) black vinegar
- 8 g honey

### Soy and Black Vinegar Pan Sauce

- 60 g chicken stock
- 10 g light soy sauce
- 8 g Shaoxing wine or sake
- 3–5 g Zhenjiang black vinegar, to taste
- 5 g cold unsalted butter

### Chili-Crisp Green Beans

- 300 g green beans, trimmed and thoroughly dried
- 10 g neutral oil
- 6 g garlic, finely minced
- 4 g fresh ginger, finely minced
- 8 g light soy sauce
- 10 g Zhenjiang black vinegar
- 12–15 g chili crisp, such as Lao Gan Ma or Fly By Jing
- 2 g sugar
- 3–4 g toasted sesame seeds, optional

### Ginger-Scallion Jasmine Rice

- 180 g jasmine rice
- 225 g water (starting amount for a covered saucepan; follow the package if it differs)
- 5 g fresh ginger, finely minced or grated
- 3 g neutral oil
- 1–2 g kosher salt, to taste
- 15 g scallions, thinly sliced

## Directions

### 1. Dry-brine the pork

Pat the pork dry. Season evenly with the salt only and refrigerate uncovered for 4–12 hours if time allows. If cooking immediately, let the salted pork sit while you prepare the remaining ingredients.

Do not apply the five-spice, ginger, garlic, or oil yet—those go on after dry-brining, immediately before roasting, so the surface stays drier for browning and fresh garlic does not sit on the meat for hours.

Combine the soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, black vinegar, and honey for the glaze. Reserve until the high-heat finish; do not apply it before the low-temperature roast.

### 2. Begin roasting the pork

Preheat the oven to 225°F convection, or 225°F in a conventional oven.

Just before the pork goes in, mix the five-spice powder with the neutral oil, ginger, and garlic. Rub this mixture over the pork.

Place the pork on a wire rack over a rimmed baking sheet. Roast until the center reaches approximately 125–130°F, beginning to check after 45 minutes. Expect roughly 50–80 minutes, depending on the roast's shape, thickness, and starting temperature.

Use an instant-read thermometer or leave-in probe positioned in the thickest part of the meat. Temperature, rather than time, determines when to proceed.

### 3. Prepare the rice

Rinse the jasmine rice until the water runs mostly clear, then drain well.

Combine the rice, 225 g water, ginger, oil, and salt in a saucepan. Bring to a simmer, cover, and cook over low heat without lifting the lid, usually about 12–15 minutes. If your rice package specifies a different water ratio or method, follow that instead and keep the ginger, oil, and salt.

Remove from the heat and let stand, still covered, for 10 minutes. Fluff gently and fold in most of the scallions, reserving some for garnish. Keep covered until serving.

### 4. Rest the pork and roast the green beans

When the pork reaches 125–130°F, remove it from the oven and rest loosely tented while the oven heats. Increase the oven temperature to 500°F convection.

If the rice is already cooked, leave it covered off the heat.

Toss the green beans with the neutral oil and arrange them on a sheet pan. Roast until just shy of tender-crisp with some browned spots, approximately 10–15 minutes depending on bean thickness, turning once. Pull them a little early; they will finish briefly in the skillet.

Time the beans around the pork rather than a fixed clock: start them while the oven preheats for the finish, or while the glazed pork is in the oven, so they are ready to coat near serving.

### 5. Glaze and finish the pork

Check the pork’s center temperature after resting. Carryover heat may have moved it closer to 145°F while the oven preheated.

Brush a thin layer of the reserved glaze over the pork. Avoid a thick coating, as the honey can burn.

If the center is already approaching 145°F, return the pork to the 500°F oven only briefly—just long enough to set and lightly brown the glaze. If it still needs more heat to reach 145°F, roast until the center is done, watching carefully. This often takes about 3–7 minutes when starting from 125–130°F, but treat that as an estimate; finish by thermometer, not by the clock.

If the glaze is browning too quickly before the center is ready, lower the oven temperature or switch to a brief broil with close supervision rather than leaving it at 500°F.

Rest for at least 3 minutes before slicing. Another 5–10 minutes of resting makes slicing easier. For safety, verify the final temperature with a thermometer; do not rely on carryover alone to reach 145°F.

### 6. Finish the green beans

While the pork finishes or rests, heat a wide skillet over medium heat. Add the roasted green beans and toss briefly—only long enough to warm them through and finish any remaining crisp-tenderness, not to cook them soft.

Reduce the heat to medium-low. Add the garlic and ginger and cook for about 30 seconds, until fragrant.

Combine the soy sauce, black vinegar, and sugar. Add to the skillet and toss for 20–30 seconds, just until the beans are lightly coated.

Remove from the heat and toss with the chili crisp. Start with 12 g and add more to taste. Finish with sesame seeds if using.

Adding chili crisp off heat helps preserve its texture and prevents the aromatic solids from scorching.

### 7. Make the pan sauce

In a small saucepan, combine the chicken stock, soy sauce, and Shaoxing wine. Simmer until lightly reduced and concentrated, approximately 3–5 minutes.

Remove from the heat and whisk in the cold butter. Add 3 g black vinegar, taste, and increase to 5 g if needed. Adjust the seasoning carefully, since the soy sauce provides salt.

If there are clean, flavorful drippings from the pork, add a small amount to the sauce before mounting with butter.

### 8. Serve

Slice the pork across the grain into approximately ¼-inch slices. Arrange with the green beans and ginger-scallion rice.

Spoon a little pan sauce over or alongside the pork. Garnish with the reserved scallions and, if desired, a few extra sesame seeds.

## Notes

- **Five-spice:** Use a restrained amount initially. Star anise and clove can dominate if the blend is particularly pungent.
- **Vinegar:** Zhenjiang black vinegar provides a malty, complex acidity. Add it to the pan sauce off heat to preserve its brightness.
- **Chili crisp:** Lao Gan Ma gives a savory, crunchy finish; Fly By Jing offers a different, more Sichuan-aromatic profile. Both work well.
- **Pork temperature:** The recommended safe endpoint for whole-muscle pork is 145°F followed by at least a 3-minute rest.
- **Timing:** The roast's dimensions and starting temperature have a greater influence on cooking time than its weight alone. Coordinate the sides around the thermometer reading rather than a fixed schedule.
- **Scaling:** The pork seasoning and glaze can be scaled proportionally. Rice and vegetable quantities can be adjusted independently to suit the number of servings.
