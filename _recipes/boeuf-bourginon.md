A classic French boeuf bourguignon adapted for a long, precisely controlled stovetop braise. Large pieces of beef chuck are lightly dusted with flour and deeply seared, then gently cooked in reduced red wine, beef stock, and aromatics at 185°F (85°C) until succulent and fork-tender.

The braising vegetables are discarded after cooking, while pearl onions, mushrooms, carrots, and lardons are prepared separately to preserve their individual textures. The strained braising liquid is reduced into a concentrated, glossy sauce before the dish is assembled.

Designed for a Breville Control Freak with a 4-quart saucier and a separate skillet for searing and finishing vegetables.

Allow approximately 8–10 hours total, including preparation, a 6–9-hour braise, and final sauce reduction. The beef's tenderness, rather than a fixed cooking time, determines when the braise is finished.

## Ingredients

### Beef

- 3 pounds (1,360 g) boneless beef chuck roast
- 25 g all-purpose flour
- 13–15 g kosher salt (approximately 1% of beef weight)
- 2 g freshly ground black pepper
- 30–45 g neutral oil or rendered beef tallow, as needed for searing

### Braising Liquid and Aromatics

- 750 g dry red wine (1 bottle)
- 400–600 g unsalted or lightly salted beef stock, preferably gelatin-rich
- 200 g yellow onion, roughly chopped
- 120 g carrot, cut into large pieces
- 15 g garlic, lightly crushed
- 25 g tomato paste
- 4 g fresh thyme
- 10 g parsley stems
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 small sprig rosemary, optional

### Finishing Vegetables and Garnishes

- 250 g pearl or cipollini onions, peeled
- 250 g carrots, peeled and cut into substantial, even pieces
- 250 g cremini or button mushrooms, cleaned and halved or quartered
- 150 g lardons or thick-cut bacon, cut into batons
- 25 g unsalted butter, divided
- 100–150 g beef stock or water, as needed for glazing vegetables
- 10–15 g flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste

## Directions

### Prepare and Season the Beef

Trim away any large exterior deposits of hard fat or silver skin, but retain the internal connective tissue and marbling.

Cut the chuck into large pieces approximately 2–2½ inches (5–6 cm) across. Aim for relatively uniform thickness to promote even cooking.

Season evenly with 13–15 g kosher salt and 2 g black pepper. For best results, place the beef on a rack over a tray and refrigerate uncovered for 4–12 hours. This allows the salt to penetrate and dries the exterior for better browning.

Before searing, pat the beef dry. Sprinkle with 25 g flour, tossing to coat the pieces very lightly. Shake off any excess. The flour should form a thin, nearly invisible coating rather than a breading.

### Reduce the Red Wine

Pour 750 g red wine into a wide saucepan or skillet.

Bring to a boil, then maintain a vigorous simmer until reduced by approximately half, yielding 375 g. This should take approximately 20–30 minutes, depending on the pan's surface area.

Reserve the reduction.

The wine should taste concentrated and savory, with some acidity remaining. Avoid reducing until syrupy or excessively tannic.

**Control Freak settings:**
- Pan control: 250°F (121°C), Fast intensity
- Adjust as necessary to maintain an active simmer
- Target final wine weight: 375 g

### Sear the Beef

Preheat a 13-inch skillet to 375°F (191°C) using pan control.

Add enough neutral oil or tallow to create a thin film across the cooking surface.

Working in batches, place the lightly floured beef into the hot skillet without crowding. Sear until deeply browned on several sides, approximately 6–8 minutes per batch.

Avoid moving the pieces prematurely. Allow the flour coating and meat surface to develop a deep brown crust, but reduce the temperature slightly if the flour begins to scorch.

Transfer the browned beef to a tray and reserve any accumulated juices.

**Control Freak settings:**
- Pan control: 375°F (191°C)
- Intensity: Fast
- Cook time: approximately 6–8 minutes per batch

### Brown the Braising Aromatics

In the same skillet, reduce the temperature to approximately 325°F (163°C).

Add the chopped yellow onion and carrot, using a small amount of additional fat if necessary. Cook for 5–7 minutes until lightly browned.

Add the crushed garlic and cook for another minute.

Stir in the tomato paste and cook for 1–2 minutes until darkened slightly and fragrant.

Add approximately 100 g of the reserved reduced wine to the skillet, scraping thoroughly to dissolve the browned fond.

Transfer the vegetables and deglazing liquid to the 4-quart saucier.

Add the remaining wine reduction, thyme, parsley stems, bay leaves, and optional rosemary.

### Assemble the Braise

Arrange the browned beef in the saucier, keeping the pieces relatively compact. Pour in any juices accumulated on the resting tray.

Add 400 g beef stock initially, distributing the liquid around the meat.

The beef should be approximately 75–90% submerged. Add additional stock in 50–100 g increments if necessary, without completely filling the saucier.

The total contents should ideally remain below approximately 3 L to allow sufficient headspace.

Bring the liquid to a gentle simmer, stirring carefully to distribute the tomato paste and flour without disturbing the browned meat excessively.

Once the liquid has reached a simmer, transition to probe control.

### Long, Low-Temperature Braise

Position the Control Freak probe so the tip is submerged in the braising liquid approximately ¼–½ inch above the bottom of the saucier. Ensure the probe does not touch the pan or rest against a piece of beef.

Cover the saucier tightly. If the probe prevents the lid from seating, use a cooking film specifically rated for prolonged exposure at the cooking temperature, secured so it cannot contact the burner or other excessively hot surfaces, with the lid positioned over it. A fitted silicone cover with a probe opening is another option.

Set the Control Freak to 185°F (85°C) in probe-control mode.

**Control Freak settings:**
- Mode: Probe control
- Liquid temperature: 185°F (85°C)
- Intensity: Slow, after the liquid reaches temperature
- Covered cook time: approximately 6–9 hours
- Begin checking tenderness after 6 hours

Maintain the liquid at 185°F throughout the braise. It should remain below a conventional simmer, with little visible bubbling.

Check the liquid level and probe position every 2–3 hours. Add small amounts of hot stock if necessary to prevent the exposed beef or aromatics from drying out.

Avoid unnecessary stirring, which can break apart the meat as it becomes tender.

After approximately 6 hours, begin testing the largest pieces with a thin skewer or fork. The skewer should pass through the meat with very little resistance, while the pieces should still retain their shape when lifted.

If the beef remains noticeably firm after 7–8 hours, increase the liquid temperature to 190–194°F (88–90°C) and continue cooking for 1–2 hours, checking periodically.

The desired result is exceptionally tender beef with softened, gelatinous connective tissue, but not meat that disintegrates into individual fibers.

### Separate the Beef and Braising Liquid

When the beef reaches the desired tenderness, turn off the heat.

Allow the beef to rest in the braising liquid for approximately 15–20 minutes.

Using a slotted spoon or tongs, carefully transfer the beef to a shallow covered dish. Handle gently to avoid breaking the pieces.

Strain the braising liquid through a fine-mesh sieve into a clean saucepan or bowl.

Discard the spent onion, carrot, garlic, herb stems, and bay leaves. These vegetables have contributed their flavor to the sauce and are not intended for serving.

Allow the strained liquid to settle briefly, then skim excess surface fat with a spoon or fat separator.

Reserve the defatted braising liquid for reduction.

### Reduce and Finish the Sauce

Transfer the strained braising liquid to a wide saucepan or skillet.

Bring to a steady simmer and reduce uncovered until the liquid becomes glossy and lightly coats the back of a spoon.

The exact reduction depends on the amount of liquid remaining and the gelatin content of the beef stock. Expect approximately 15–30 minutes, though a larger volume may require longer.

Stir occasionally and skim any foam or excess fat that rises to the surface.

The flour used during searing and gelatin extracted from the chuck should provide some natural body. Avoid adding additional flour unless absolutely necessary.

If the sauce becomes too thick, loosen with a small amount of hot beef stock. If too thin, continue reducing.

Taste and adjust with salt and black pepper only after the sauce reaches its final concentration.

If the wine character is excessively sharp, a small amount of butter can soften the sauce's texture and perceived acidity.

**Control Freak settings:**
- Pan control: 215–225°F (102–107°C)
- Intensity: Fast
- Cook time: approximately 15–30 minutes, or until the desired consistency is reached

### Prepare the Lardons

While the sauce reduces, place the lardons into a cool skillet.

Set the Control Freak to approximately 300°F (149°C) and cook gradually, allowing the fat to render.

Continue until the lardons are golden brown and lightly crisp, approximately 8–12 minutes.

Remove with a slotted spoon and reserve.

Leave the rendered fat in the skillet for cooking the finishing vegetables.

### Sauté the Mushrooms

Increase the skillet temperature to approximately 350°F (177°C).

Add the mushrooms to the rendered lardon fat, working in batches if necessary to avoid crowding.

Cook until their moisture has evaporated and the cut surfaces become deeply golden brown, approximately 6–10 minutes.

Add approximately 10 g butter toward the end and toss to coat.

Season lightly with salt and pepper, then remove and reserve.

The mushrooms should be well browned and tender, not stewed in the sauce.

### Glaze the Pearl Onions

Reduce the skillet temperature to approximately 325°F (163°C).

Add the peeled pearl or cipollini onions and approximately 10 g butter, using some of the remaining lardon fat.

Cook until lightly golden on several sides, approximately 5–7 minutes.

Add 50–75 g beef stock or water and a small pinch of salt.

Cover and cook gently for 10–15 minutes, or until the onions are tender throughout.

Remove the cover and allow the liquid to reduce until it forms a light glaze around the onions.

Transfer to a bowl and reserve.

### Glaze the Carrots

Place the finishing carrots into a skillet or small saucepan with approximately 50–75 g beef stock or water, the remaining 5 g butter, and a pinch of salt.

Cover and cook at a gentle simmer until the carrots are tender but retain their shape, approximately 10–15 minutes depending on thickness.

Uncover and reduce any remaining liquid until the carrots are lightly glazed.

Avoid overcooking. The carrots should provide a distinct, slightly firmer texture alongside the very tender beef.

### Assemble the Bourguignon

Return the reserved beef to the cleaned 4-quart saucier.

Pour the finished red wine sauce over the beef and gently turn the pieces to coat.

Warm over low heat until the beef is heated through, keeping the sauce below a simmer to avoid further breaking down the meat.

**Control Freak settings:**
- Probe control: 165–170°F (74–77°C), if the probe can be positioned properly
- Alternatively, use gentle pan control and monitor the sauce
- Cook time: approximately 10–15 minutes, or until heated through

Add the glazed pearl onions, carrots, and sautéed mushrooms. Fold gently into the sauce, taking care to preserve the shape of the beef and vegetables.

Add the crisp lardons immediately before serving, reserving a small amount for garnish if desired.

Finish with finely chopped parsley and freshly ground black pepper.

Serve in warmed shallow bowls with enough sauce to coat the beef generously.

The bourguignon pairs particularly well with pommes purée, buttered egg noodles, or crusty bread.

## Notes

### Beef Texture and Temperature

- The 185°F (85°C) braising temperature is intended to promote gradual collagen conversion while limiting the aggressive muscle-fiber contraction associated with near-boiling braises.
- Expect approximately 6–9 hours for large pieces of chuck, but connective tissue content and cut thickness will affect the required time.
- A finished piece should yield readily to a skewer while remaining intact when lifted.
- If the beef remains firm after an extended hold, increasing the temperature to 190–194°F (88–90°C) can accelerate tenderization.
- Avoid a vigorous simmer during the long braise.

### Flour and Sauce Texture

- The 25 g flour coating is intentionally lighter than a traditional flour-thickened stew.
- Most of the final sauce body should come from reduced stock, dissolved gelatin, and a small amount of starch from the seared flour.
- Reducing the strained liquid separately allows precise control over sauce consistency without overcooking the beef.
- Gelatin-rich homemade beef stock is preferable to thin commercial stock.

### Wine Selection

- Choose a dry, moderately tannic red wine such as Pinot Noir, Côtes du Rhône, or an unoaked or lightly oaked blend.
- Reducing the wine by half before braising concentrates its flavor without making it excessively syrupy.
- Because the braise remains tightly covered, relatively little additional wine reduction occurs during the long cook.
- Final sauce reduction will concentrate acidity, tannins, and salt, so adjust seasoning only after reducing.

### Vegetables and Garnishes

- The onion, carrot, and garlic used during braising are sacrificial aromatics and should be discarded.
- Separately cooking the finishing vegetables preserves their distinct textures, colors, and flavors.
- The pearl onions should be tender and glazed, mushrooms deeply browned, and carrots tender but structured.
- Add the lardons near serving time to preserve some crispness.

### Equipment and Capacity

- The 4-quart saucier is suitable for approximately 1.36 kg chuck if the meat is arranged compactly and the braising liquid is carefully controlled.
- Start with 400 g stock and add only what is needed to mostly submerge the beef.
- Use a larger skillet for searing to avoid crowding.
- For the covered braise, use only cooking film rated for the intended temperature and duration, keeping it away from the induction surface and other hot exterior surfaces.
- The probe tip must remain submerged in liquid without contacting the pan or meat.

### Make-Ahead and Storage

- Bourguignon benefits from overnight refrigeration, allowing the flavors to integrate and making excess fat easier to remove.
- For make-ahead preparation, cool the beef and braising liquid rapidly in shallow containers and refrigerate separately from the finishing vegetables.
- Refrigerate promptly, within 2 hours of completing cooking.
- The following day, remove solidified fat, reduce the sauce, and rewarm the beef gently before adding freshly prepared garnishes.
- Refrigerate leftovers for up to 3–4 days or freeze the beef and sauce for up to 3 months for best quality.
- Reheat leftovers thoroughly to 165°F (74°C).
