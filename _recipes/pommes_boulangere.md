## Ingredients

### Potatoes
- 300 g yellow potatoes (Yukon Gold)
- 100 g yellow onion
- 25 g unsalted butter
- 5 g olive oil
- 125–150 g homemade chicken stock
- 1–2 g fresh thyme leaves
- 3–4 g kosher salt, to taste
- Freshly ground black pepper, to taste

## Equipment
- 1 pint (2-cup) oven-safe baking dish
- Mandoline
- Small skillet
- Parchment paper for cartouche
- Foil
- Small oven-safe plate or other suitable weight

## Method

### 1. Caramelize the Onions

1. Thinly slice the onion, approximately 2 mm thick.
2. Heat olive oil and 10 g of butter in a small skillet over medium-low heat.
3. Add onions and a pinch of salt.
4. Cook gently for 25–35 minutes, stirring periodically, until deeply golden, soft, and sweet.
5. Avoid burning or developing black bits, which will introduce bitterness.
6. Set aside.

### 2. Prepare the Potatoes

1. Peel the potatoes if desired.
2. Using a mandoline, slice potatoes approximately 2 mm thick.
3. Do not rinse the potatoes after slicing. Retain the surface starch to help bind the layers.
4. Melt the remaining 15 g butter.

### 3. Assemble the Dish

1. Lightly butter the baking dish.
2. Spread the caramelized onions evenly across the bottom.
3. Arrange a layer of overlapping potato slices over the onions.
4. Season lightly with salt and black pepper.
5. Drizzle with melted butter and sprinkle with thyme.
6. Continue layering potatoes, seasoning, butter, and thyme, alternating the direction of the slices.
7. Finish with a neat, overlapping layer of potatoes.
8. Pour approximately 125 g chicken stock into the dish. The liquid should come partway up the potato layers without completely submerging them.

### 4. Prepare the Cartouche

1. Cut a piece of parchment paper to fit the baking dish.
2. Make a small hole in the center to allow some steam to escape.
3. Place the cartouche directly over the potatoes.
4. Cover with foil.
5. Place a suitably sized oven-safe plate on top to gently weight the potatoes and encourage the layers to settle.

### 5. Initial Bake

1. Preheat oven to 250°F.
2. Bake the covered and weighted dish for approximately 60–75 minutes.
3. Check the potatoes with a thin knife. They should be tender and beginning to bind together.
4. If necessary, continue cooking until the potatoes are fully tender.

### 6. Low-Temperature Finish

1. Remove the foil, weighted plate, and cartouche.
2. Maintain the oven temperature at 265°F.
3. Continue baking uncovered for approximately 20–25 minutes.
4. Allow the stock to reduce and the potato layers to settle.
5. Avoid increasing the oven temperature. Excessive heat can cause the exposed top layer to lose moisture, shrink, and shrivel.
6. Finish with a light brushing of melted butter and a little fresh thyme, if desired.

### 7. Hold and Serve

1. Remove from the oven and allow the dish to settle briefly.
2. Serve warm alongside roast chicken and pan jus.

**Make-ahead and holding:** The dish can be prepared and baked ahead of the chicken. Keep the total time at room temperature, including cooling, under 2 hours. For longer holding, refrigerate promptly and reheat thoroughly before serving.

## Notes

- **Potatoes:** Yukon Gold potatoes provide a creamy texture while retaining enough structure to maintain distinct layers.
- **Stock:** Homemade chicken stock complements roast chicken particularly well. Brown chicken stock is also excellent for a deeper, more savory result.
- **Butter:** Ordinary unsalted butter is sufficient; clarification is unnecessary at these cooking temperatures.
- **Stock quantity:** Start with 125 g. Add a little more if the potatoes appear dry during assembly.
- **Texture:** The objective is tender, cohesive layers with a gently finished top, rather than a crispy gratin crust.
- **Service:** Particularly good with poulet rôti, roasted chicken drippings, and a concentrated chicken pan jus.
